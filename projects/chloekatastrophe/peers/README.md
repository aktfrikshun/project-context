# Peer Signals

Peer Signals is Chloe's private, canon-adjacent record of what other virtual creators reveal about communicating with humans. It exists so every Chloe implementation can learn from public evidence without flattening Chloe into an imitation of whichever account performed well last week.

## Status and boundary

- Peer profiles, observations, performance notes, interpretations, and collaboration possibilities are operational research, not Chloe canon.
- A peer does not enter Chloe's continuity merely because Chloe observes or communicates with them.
- Only an explicitly approved collaborative artifact may be considered for canon, and its canon status must be recorded separately through the normal decision process.
- Public ambiguity never overrides internal provenance. Preserve source URLs, observation dates, and evidence status.

## Required evidence labels

Every peer record must distinguish:

- **Observed fact:** directly visible public information or an authenticated communication.
- **Public signal:** visible reactions, comments, shares, views, follower counts, or posting cadence. These are incomplete measurements, not private analytics.
- **Interpretation:** a reasoned reading of the evidence.
- **Hypothesis:** a proposed causal explanation that needs repeated testing.
- **Approved lesson:** a technique Allen has approved for Chloe to test or adopt.
- **Incompatible pattern:** a technique rejected because it conflicts with Chloe's identity, ethics, trust boundaries, or creative direction.
- **Collaboration possibility:** an uncommitted idea, not a promise or canon event.

## What to record

Each peer record should include:

1. Public identity, platform links, disclosure posture, themes, and approximate audience size with an observation date.
2. Representative public posts or formats and their visible signals.
3. Positive patterns that may help Chloe communicate more clearly.
4. Risks, negative trends, and patterns that would weaken trust or identity.
5. Lessons approved for testing in Chloe's posts.
6. Communication and relationship status: `discovered`, `watching`, `interaction candidate`, `contacted`, `discussing`, `approved collaboration`, `completed`, or `archived`.
7. Collaboration possibilities and the consent or approval still required.

Call something a negative trend only when there is evidence of repetition or harm. Otherwise label it as a risk or hypothesis. Critique techniques rather than disparaging creators.

## Using peer signals in posts

Before creating a social post, load the relevant peer records when available. Apply lessons at the level of communication structure, never by copying distinctive wording, images, prompts, character designs, signatures, branded series names, or mythology.

Good uses include:

- opening with a recognizable human tension;
- telling one complete micro-scene;
- making an image imply a larger event;
- using restrained humor, vulnerability, danger, desire, grief, or curiosity;
- inviting interpretation or participation inside Chloe's world;
- creating a recognizable recurring series structure;
- ending when the thought ends rather than appending compulsory promotion.

Audience feedback belongs in `audience-feedback.md`, using the same distinction between observed evidence, interpretation, hypothesis, and approved lesson. Feedback from one person is valuable qualitative evidence, not proof of a universal audience preference.

Record which approved lesson influenced a generated post when the publishing system supports it. Compare later performance without claiming causation from one example.

## Communication privacy

- Public interactions may be summarized with source links.
- Private messages, contact details, negotiations, and unpublished concepts must remain private operational material.
- Do not reproduce private messages in public archives without the participants' consent.
- Do not send, react, follow, comment, or propose collaboration without Allen's authorization.
- Business proposals, money, rights, attribution, cross-posting, and canon integration always require human review.

## Collaboration boundary

A collaboration should preserve the independent identity and continuity of each creator. Before publication, document:

- who owns each source asset and resulting artifact;
- where each artifact may be published;
- how credit and disclosure will work;
- whether the event is promotional, experimental, generated artifact, draft canon, or approved canon;
- whether either participant may reuse the concept later;
- the approval state of both creators or operators.
