# Business and Canon Rules

1. Every meaningful item carries a status: confirmed canon, approved decision/model, draft canon, proposal, generated artifact, recovered fragment, contradiction, unresolved mystery, or deprecated material.
2. Only approved/reference canon is usable for generation by default; unresolved mysteries are excluded.
3. Public visibility and canonical approval are separate decisions.
4. Generated media accidents and fan theories never become canon automatically.
5. Chloe Model v1 governs new visual production unless an older archive style is explicitly requested.
6. Public archive and private creator operations remain separate surfaces.
7. The public archive exposes approved artifacts; private drafts, credentials, and operational data remain private.
8. Rails/public archive owns durable shared artifact/release/search schema; Creator OS treats it as a contract.
9. Ordinary social posts should be native and conversational, not burdened by compulsory promotional footers; dedicated participation messages carry broader invitations.
10. Social posts are concise signals, not compressed archive entries. When an artifact needs provenance, context, contradiction, or extended reflection, preserve that depth in the FrikShun marketing archive and let the social post open the door to it.
11. Keep the public archive active at the same cadence as Chloe's social presence: each socially active day creates or updates an artifact record, field note, daily signal log, or collaboration record. This is daily continuity, not compulsory one-entry-per-post duplication.
12. Recovered-memory and recovered-artifact social posts display the stable public catalog entry ID shared with their archive record; questions, musings, and other non-recovery families do not borrow that marker.
13. Automatic daily generation randomizes among families not used in the recent daily history and stores the selected family explicitly. Manual family selection may override rotation intentionally.
14. Daily imagery independently rotates among full-body action, environmental story, fine art, and editorial portrait modes. Store the selected mode explicitly and avoid recently used modes and overused mirror/duplicate-Chloe compositions unless the subject requires them.
15. Artistic ambiguity cannot override disclosure, consent, payment, safety, or privacy requirements.
